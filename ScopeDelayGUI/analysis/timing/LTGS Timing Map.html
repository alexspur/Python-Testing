How to read it

  • Each row is one point a trigger edge passes. The rows of a group run in the order the edge gets there.
  • A row's trace steps up when the edge arrives. The first number on the row is that arrival time after T0.
  • The equation is how that time is built: the row it follows, plus one hop. A dotted line joins the two rows. The thick bar is the hop.
  • A hop is one of three things: a cable, a fiber link, or the delay inside an instrument from its trigger input to its output.
  • Programmed delays are at zero. In a shot, add 180 µs to every row from the screen room DG535 output on, and 3.025 µs to every laser row.

Example: Scope 2 EXT TRIG reads 8.933 = 4.471 + 4.462. The edge reaches scope 1 at 4.471 ns. It needs 4.462 ns more on the next cable to reach scope 2.

Why three scope cables

There is one trigger output for all three scopes. A tee at each scope's EXT input passes the edge on to the next scope, and scope 3 ends the chain in 50 Ω. So the edge reaches the scopes one after the other, and each arrival adds one cable to the one before.

The three cables were replaced on 5 October 2026 at about noon. Shots before that used longer cables of 4.599, 4.604 and 9.188 ns, so the old arrivals are 4.599, 9.203 and 18.391 ns. The diagram shows the new set. The analysis scripts pick the set from the shot date.

The sync output is taken from the solid state amplifier at the front of the generator. The thyratron and the two spark gaps come after it. So the Trig Sync signal on scope 1 CH4 shows when the amplifier fired. It does not show the 1.7 to 2.7 ns of jitter on the high voltage pulse.

The sync delay of 33 ns sits inside the 80 ns manual limit. The spike at the Marx arrives 243.5 ns after the trigger input. The check sheet gives 223.5 ns at the end of a 20 ft output cable, so the spike is 20 ns later than that figure. A longer output cable, a different switch pressure, or a monitor that responds to the Marx gap would each explain it. These are single shot values, good to about 2 ns, and the generator itself moves by 1.7 to 2.7 ns from shot to shot.

With zero programmed delay, the trigger reaches the TG-100 at 220.7 ns after T0. The check sheet delay puts the high voltage pulse at the end of the 20 ft output cable at 444.2 ns.

Not measured yet

  • The programmed delays on laser DG535 channels A to D are not on this page. Neither are the flashlamp and Q-switch delays inside the lasers.
  • Only CH4 on each scope has a measured offset. Channels 1 to 3 borrow it. Channel skew on scope 1 measured 18 ps, so the risk is small.
  • The offsets were taken at 50 Ω. The campaign runs 1 MΩ with a 50 Ω terminator and a 20 dB attenuator. That mismatch showed up as about 1.6 ns on scope 1 CH4.
  • The 33 ns sync delay and the 243.5 ns to the Marx spike come from one shot in July. More shots would give their spread.
  • The TG-100 path TF1 was bench tested with a BNC 575 pulse. In service the TG-100 drives it, and a different pulse amplitude can change its delay and jitter.
  • That total reads 162.38 ns from 50 shots at 1 MΩ and 164.52 ns from one shot at 50 Ω. A 50 shot run at 50 Ω settles it.
  • The 89.989 ns screen room insertion delay is for the rear A output. Confirm the 84 ft cable leaves from that same connector.
  • The high voltage output timing comes from the 2020 check sheet at 44 psi. A measurement at the present pressure would replace it. The path from the output cable to the switch is not included.

Sources. Scope trigger cables, the DG535 trigger cable, the 84 ft run and the two laser DG535 cables use phase slope fits of the NanoVNA sweeps. Every other cable uses half of the two way transit time in the channel table. Scope offsets come from the EXT trigger calibration. Fiber link totals come from 50 shot edge timing runs on scope 1 CH4 and scope 3 CH2. The channel A to B skew of −0.783 ns comes from the swap and average measurement in September.
